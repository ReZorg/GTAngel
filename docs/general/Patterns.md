## Overview

Peter Winiwarter's Periodic System of System Concepts is a formal classification of the organizational primitives that recur isomorphically across all self-organizing systems — from nuclear reactions to propositional logic to neural networks to linguistic computation. The system defines a **conceptual space** with unique coordinates for every fundamental organizational concept.

The coordinate system has three axes:

- **7 Conceptual Categories** (the organizational metric): existence, distinction, disjunction, conjunction, transition/branching, modular closure, self-similar recursion
- **3 Conceptual Dimensions**: structure (space), process (time), regulation (causality)
- **2 Organizational Domains**: local bottom-up integration (∫) and global top-down differentiation (∂)

This yields **7 × 3 × 2 = 42 base coordinates**. Winiwarter's 1999 Semiotica paper extends to **48 basic system concepts** by splitting certain categories across the domain boundary and adding formal logic/set-theory dual notation. The 1986 paper identifies **55 fundamental organizational isomorphies** by including expanded synonym lists for each coordinate.

The system is **self-similar**: through modular recursion (category 7), the entire table applies at every hierarchical level, with concepts reappearing periodically — hence "periodic system," in direct analogy with the periodic table of chemical elements.

## Part II: The Extended 48 Concepts (Semiotica 1999)

Winiwarter's 1999 Semiotica paper extends the base 42 coordinates to **48 basic system concepts** by adding formal dual notation for each category (logic operator / set theory operator) and by treating the domain split (local/global) as yielding distinct formal symbols rather than subscripts. The 6 additional concepts arise from the explicit formalization of:

1. **∃ and ∈ as distinct operators** (existence in logic vs. membership in set theory) applied across the domain boundary
2. **The ¬/∉ pair** where negation and non-membership carry distinct formal weight
3. **The ∀/∅ pair** where universal quantification and the empty set (opacity of lower-level content) are treated as separate formal operators

The 48 concepts are arranged in Figure 3 of the Semiotica paper as a **6 × 8 grid** (actually 7 categories × 2 domains with 3 dimensions each, plus the domain headers and dimension subscripts):

| | ∫.x (local structure) | ∫.t (local process) | ∫.c (local regulation) | ∂.x (global structure) | ∂.t (global process) | ∂.c (global regulation) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Existence** | ∃ₓ | ∃ₜ | ∃c | ∂ₓ (∈ₓ) | ∂ₜ (∈ₜ) | ∂c (∈c) |
| **2. Distinction** | ¬ₓ | ¬ₜ | ¬c | (¬∂)ₓ (∉ₓ) | (¬∂)ₜ (∉ₜ) | (¬∂)c (∉c) |
| **3. Disjunction** | ∨ₓ | ∨ₜ | ∨c | (∨∂)ₓ (∩ₓ) | (∨∂)ₜ (∩ₜ) | (∨∂)c (∩c) |
| **4. Conjunction** | ∧ₓ | ∧ₜ | ∧c | (∧∂)ₓ (∪ₓ) | (∧∂)ₜ (∪ₜ) | (∧∂)c (∪c) |
| **5. Transition** | ⇒ₓ | ⇒ₜ | ⇒c | (⇒∂)ₓ (⊂ₓ) | (⇒∂)ₜ (⊂ₜ) | (⇒∂)c (⊂c) |
| **6. Closure** | ⇔ₓ | ⇔ₜ | ⇔c | (⇔∂)ₓ (⊂ₓ) | (⇔∂)ₜ (⊂ₜ) | (⇔∂)c (⊂c) |
| **7. Recursion** | ∀ₓ | ∀ₜ | ∀c | (∀∂)ₓ (∅ₓ) | (∀∂)ₜ (∅ₜ) | (∀∂)c (∅c) |

The 6 additional concepts (48 − 42 = 6) emerge from the formal distinction between the logic operator and its set-theory dual at the recursion level, where the operators have maximally divergent interpretations:

- ∀ₓ (for all structural modules) vs. ∅ₓ (structural opacity — the content becomes invisible)
- ∀ₜ (for all temporal cycles) vs. ∅ₜ (temporal opacity — the internal process becomes a black box)
- ∀c (for all causal loops) vs. ∅c (causal opacity — the internal regulation becomes a given)

These 6 additional concepts capture the **Janus-faced** character of modular recursion (Koestler): looking *down*, the module is a universe containing everything (∀); looking *up*, the module is an opaque atom with no visible interior (∅).
